LinkWary

About this tool

LinkWary is a free one-page link safety checker for anyone who received a suspicious link by email, SMS or WhatsApp. It breaks the address into its parts, shows you the real domain, looks up how long ago that domain was registered and when it expires, and lists the phishing red flags it finds.

It exists because the single most common way people get deceived online is not a hacked account or a clever piece of malware. It is a link that looks right.

The problem it solves

People judge a web address by how it starts, and scammers know it. An address such as https://paypal.com.secure-login.xyz/verify starts with PayPal and ends with a page called verify, so at a glance it reads as genuine. The part of that address that decides where you actually go is secure-login.xyz. Everything to its left is a label invented by whoever owns that domain.

Reading a URL correctly takes about ten seconds of concentration that nobody has when a message arrives claiming their parcel is stuck at customs or their account is about to be suspended. This tool does the reading for you.

How it works

  1. You paste an address. Your browser splits it into scheme, username, subdomain, registered domain, port, path and query string, and marks the one part that matters.
  2. A fixed list of structural checks runs on your own device: hidden usernames, lookalike characters, brand names being copied, brand names borrowed by a subdomain, raw IP addresses, non-standard ports, unencrypted connections, shortened links, hyphen stacking, urgency wording and bulk-registration extensions.
  3. Your browser then asks the public RDAP registry directory for the registered domain. The reply gives the registration date, the expiry date, the registrar and whether DNSSEC is enabled. Domain age is the strongest free signal available, because phishing domains are overwhelmingly registered days before they are used.
  4. You get a verdict of Looks safe, Be careful or Danger signs, with the reason for it written out in plain words.

Where the data comes from

Registration date, expiry date, registrar, DNSSECThe public RDAP protocol, via the free directory at rdap.org, which redirects to the registry responsible for that extension. RDAP replaced WHOIS and is published by the registries themselves.

What this tool cannot tell you. It does not connect to the website you are checking. It does not scan the destination for malware. It cannot recognise a fake login form. It does not query real-time blocklists of known malicious addresses. A domain that is fifteen years old can still be compromised and used to serve a scam page. A clean result here means "no structural warning signs", not "this is safe". Keep browser warnings enabled, keep antivirus software updated, and when a message claims to be from your bank, type the bank's address yourself.

Privacy

The link you paste is never sent to this website. Every check runs in your own browser. The only network request is a lookup of the registered domain name against the public registry directory. See the privacy policy for the full detail, including how advertising on this site is handled.

Some registries publish nothing

Not every country extension operates a free RDAP service. When the registry for a domain has none, this tool reports that the record is unavailable rather than guessing, because a missing record is not the same thing as a safe one.
