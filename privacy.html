Privacy policy

This page explains exactly what happens to a link you paste into LinkWary. There is no account, no cookie consent banner, and no profile of you, because there is nothing to build one from.

What we do not do

  • We do not store the links you check. There is no database behind this site.
  • We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number or payment details.
  • We do not require an account or a login of any kind.
  • We do not sell, rent or share personal information, because we do not collect any.
  • We do not visit, fetch or connect to the website you are checking. We only analyse the text of the address.

What runs on your own device

When you paste a link and press check, the address is split into its parts and every warning-sign check is performed by JavaScript running in your own browser. The full address — including its path, its query string and any token it carries — never leaves your device and is never transmitted to this website or to any server we control.

The one external request we make

To find out how old a domain is, your browser sends a request to the public RDAP registry directory at rdap.org, which forwards it to the registry responsible for that domain extension. That request contains only the registered domain name — for example example.com — and not the rest of the link you pasted.

RDAP is a free public standard operated by the domain registries themselves. Like any web request, the registry service may log the request together with standard technical information such as your IP address and the time of the request, under their own privacy policy. We have no control over those logs and we receive no data back from them other than the public registration record, which is shown to you on screen.

Accuracy and liability

LinkWary reports structural warning signs and publicly available domain registration records. A result of "looks safe" means no such warning signs were found. It is not a guarantee that a destination website is honest, safe or free of malicious content, and you should not rely on it as security advice.
